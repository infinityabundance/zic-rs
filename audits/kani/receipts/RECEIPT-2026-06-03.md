## What this closes / does not close

- **Closes (predicate level):** the four standards-precision invariants previously tracked as *unchecked*
  gaps in `../claim-boundary-map.md` now have a **machine-proven predicate** (`memory-safe ≠ format-valid`:
  these are the format-validity rules `parse` is intentionally lenient about).
- **Closes (enforcement) — done same arc (T23.kani.3f.enforce):** the proven predicates are now **wired into
  `rfc9636::validate`**, which reads the raw `isdst`/`desigidx`/indicator bytes (newly captured in
  `ParsedTzif::raw`) and rejects `isdst=2` / `desigidx==charcnt` / `<charcnt`-no-NUL / `charcnt==0` /
  indicator∉{0,1} / `isut=1,isstd=0` / `utoff=i32::MIN` as structural `Violation`s. 7 tests assert each
  **parses (memory-safe) yet validates as a `Violation` (format-invalid)**; real zones + reference-`zic`
  output stay conformant (the producer-profile guard). `parse` itself stays memory-safe-*lenient*.
- **Does NOT prove:** semantic correctness, civil-time truth, reference-`zic` parity, or anything beyond the
  stated per-helper property.

## Discarded approach (recorded as a method note, not a result)

An earlier broad harness that drove the **whole `parse`** over a populated symbolic body did not converge
(CBMC allocator/`format!` modeling). That is a **harness-design failure, not an audit verdict** — it is **not
counted** among the proofs and carries **no** "inconclusive"/"timeout" status on any result surface. The
lesson: Kani work here stays reduced-surface only.
