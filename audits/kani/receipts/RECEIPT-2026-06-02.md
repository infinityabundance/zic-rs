## T23.kani.3b — abbreviation-index **slice-safety** guard ✅ VERIFICATION SUCCESSFUL

`read_cstr`'s guard extracted into a pure helper `abbr_index_slice_safe(idx, table_len) = idx <= table_len`.

- **Command:** `cargo kani --harness abbr_index_guard_prevents_oob_slice`. **Result: 0 of 15 failed, 0.07 s.**
  (Raw: `raw-T23kani3b-abbr-index-guard-2026-06-02.txt`.)
- **Proves (exactly):** when the guard accepts, **`&table[idx..]` is an in-bounds (possibly empty) slice** —
  so `read_cstr` cannot panic / OOB-read on a hostile `ttinfo` designation index; when it rejects, `idx >
  table.len()`.
- **⚠ Precision boundary — this proves Rust SLICE SAFETY, NOT RFC 9636 designation-index VALIDITY.** The
  stricter RFC rule (`desigidx < charcnt` + NUL at/after + `charcnt != 0`) is **separately proven** as a
  predicate on 2026-06-03 (`RECEIPT-2026-06-03.md`, T23.kani.3f.1, shown to strictly imply this slice-safety);
  *enforcing* it in `rfc9636::validate` is the tracked next step (`../claim-boundary-map.md`). *Memory-safe
  acceptance ≠ format-valid acceptance.*

## Boundary — what these proofs do NOT prove

semantic correctness · full RFC-9636 validity · reference-`zic` parity · inputs longer than the bound ·
resource-exhaustion freedom (the input-size tail is `RISK.RESOURCE.1`, not a CPU bound) · filesystem safety.
Kani targets reduced-surface helpers, not the broad `parse` entrypoint. A bounded proof is bounded.
